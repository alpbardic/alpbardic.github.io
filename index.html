About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Chicago, advised by Prof. Erdem Koyuncu. I work on distributed and decentralized machine learning.

One question drives most of my work: how do you train and run a model on many small devices, when each device has limited compute, memory, and bandwidth, and there is no central server to coordinate them?

So far I have two main results. Random Walking Snakes is a decentralized training method that converges faster than Random Walk Learning. Decentralized Model-distributed inference splits one model across many devices and uses graph coloring to schedule them without conflicts, which lowers communication latency in a decentralized manner.

Right now I am working on wildfire hotspot detection and distributed world models. I also teach as a graduate assistant for machine learning, deep learning, and pattern recognition.

If you work on similar problems, write to me. I am glad to talk.
