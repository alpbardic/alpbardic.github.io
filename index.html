About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Chicago, advised by Prof. Erdem Koyuncu. I work on decentralized and federated learning, and on fine-tuning large language models across many devices.

How do you train one model across many devices when each holds different data, no device is in charge, and some take part far less often than others?

When participation is uneven, the model drifts toward the data of the devices it hears from most. My current work studies how this bias arises in gossip-based LLM fine-tuning and how to correct it using only local information. I am also working on distributed world models, and I teach as a graduate assistant for machine learning, deep learning, and pattern recognition.

Earlier, I built Random Walking Snakes, a decentralized training method that converges faster than Random Walk Learning, and extended it to heterogeneous data. Decentralized model-distributed inference splits one model across many devices and uses graph coloring to keep their transfers from colliding, which lowers communication latency.

If you work on similar problems, write to me. I am glad to talk.
